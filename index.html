About Research Papers Conferences Mathematical Notes Other Interests

I am a Senior R&D Engineer at Ansys, part of Synopsys, where I develop mesh generation algorithms for engineering simulation. I hold an M.S. in Mechanical Engineering from Stanford University and an M.Eng. in Electrical and Computer Engineering from the University of Alberta.

My research interests center on nonlinear finite element methods and structure-preserving algorithms for nonlinear dynamics, including symplectic algorithms and energy-momentum conserving methods. I am particularly interested in the algebraic and topological structures underlying these methods, as well as their stability and convergence. I also bring practical experience in implementing numerical methods and applying them to solve real-world problems in mechanics.

Mathematical Notes

I also enjoy pure mathematics, especially topology and geometry. I have studied point-set topology, algebraic topology, smooth manifolds, and Riemannian geometry. In my study notes, I work through definitions and proofs in detail.

  • Functional Analysis, PDE, and Finite Element Methods (PDF, 18 pages · August 2026 · Ongoing notes)

    Reading notes on Peter Monk's Finite Element Methods for Maxwell's Equations, covering variational formulations, inf-sup stability, mixed finite element methods, and Sobolev spaces. These notes support my current FEEC-related research and are being expanded and updated.

  • Simplicial Complexes and Simplicial Homology (PDF, 27 pages · March 2025 · Ongoing notes)

    Notes on weak topology, abstract simplicial complexes and their geometric realizations, and ordered and oriented simplicial homology, including their relationship with singular homology. These notes are being expanded and updated.

  • The Acyclic Models Theorem (PDF, 4 pages · March 2025)

    A companion note on models and expressible functors, with proofs of the acyclic models constructions for extending natural chain maps and chain homotopies, following Eilenberg and Mac Lane's Acyclic Models (American Journal of Mathematics, 75(1), 189–199, 1953).
